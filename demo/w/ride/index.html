What the app actually is

K·A·M RIDE is the bike computer I ride with. It is an Android app, written in Kotlin with Jetpack Compose, that records rides from the phone's GPS and exports them as GPX and TCX files, so a ride opens in any training app.

On the handlebars, the screen shows speed, time moving, distance, average speed and gradient in large pixel digits that stay readable in sunlight, with an elevation profile, a map page and big glove-sized buttons. It works with no signal: maps of the UK are stored on the phone, and routes are planned on the device.

On top of the numbers sits a small game layer, driven by the real ride, not by buttons. A buddy character rides along. A long climb brings out a boss, and you beat it by climbing. A fast stretch on the flat sets off TURBO. Coins earned by riding buy gear for the buddy in a shop. Pop-ups are spoken aloud, and the games are played by riding, never by touching the screen while moving.

How to play
  • Power on: any key, or tap the screen.
  • Start: Space, Enter, or tap (the A button on a phone).
  • Ride: Space or ↑ to jump the cones and rocks; ← → to slow down or speed up. Coins are worth 10.
  • Climb boss: pedal by tapping Space (or holding →). Climbing charges your shots, and the boss's health is the metres left to climb, as in the app. Stop pedalling and it gets away.
  • Turbo: hold → to ride 20% above your usual pace. On the turbo road, ← → steer and Space pedals harder.
  • Sound: M, or the SOUND button.
