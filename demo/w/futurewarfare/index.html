What the software actually is
  • A learning game in my Command dashboard's academy. Fifteen domains of modern conflict, each with five zones.
  • Two lenses on every domain: offence and defence (for Civilian Survival, react and prepare), so each topic is studied from both sides.
  • An AI tutor: choosing a zone sends it to my AI learning workstation, which teaches it and narrates the consequences of decisions.
  • Gamified drills: Network Defender (apply firewall, sign-in or patch defences as an attack advances), Scenario Engine (three-step branching decisions judged against civil-emergency guidance) and Deepfake Court (judge clips real or synthetic and give a forensic reason).
  • Progress that counts: XP, zones and drills feed my skills and career log, which exports as CV lines.
  • Planned: drone and pilot simulators with a phone as the controller.
Where it came from
  1. APRIL 2026A suspected security incident on my own machines. It turned out to be a false alarm.
  2. THE LESSONChecking it properly showed me how little I knew about defending my own setup.
  3. THE RESPONSEI wrote a cybersecurity curriculum and practised it against my own machines.
  4. NOWThat grew into Future Warfare: a curriculum on modern conflict for civilians and students, taught through drills: a learning game in my Command dashboard’s academy.
